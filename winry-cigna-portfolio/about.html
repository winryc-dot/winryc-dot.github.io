About me

I'm Winry Cigna, a mechanical engineering student at the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa, expecting to graduate in May 2027 with a 3.67 GPA. My work spans robotics, manufacturing, and CAD design, and I like projects that move from a drawing to something I can actually hold and test.

Most recently I worked as a Systems Engineering Intern at Booz Allen Hamilton, where I built a digital twin in Blender and used Python and AI-assisted tools to test a SLAM program for autonomous navigation, alongside a team building an autonomous drone swarming simulation in NVIDIA Isaac Sim.

I'm also the Chassis Engineer for Team Robotic Space Exploration (RoSE), a University Rover Challenge team advised by a NASA JPL researcher. I design and fabricate a modular, weather-resistant rover chassis using CAD, then bring it to life with a waterjet, hydraulic press, 3D printer, band saw, drill press, and grinder — applying statistical and tolerance analysis to make sure everything actually fits together.

Before that, I interned in construction engineering at Jas W. Glover Ltd., supporting bid estimating and reviewing engineering drawings and project specifications. I've also spent two years as a math tutor and learning assistant at the University of Hawaiʻi, teaching Algebra through Calculus IV — work that keeps me sharp at explaining technical ideas clearly, which carries over directly into engineering communication.

I'm currently looking for internship and full-time opportunities in mechanical engineering, especially roles involving robotics, hardware design, or manufacturing.
